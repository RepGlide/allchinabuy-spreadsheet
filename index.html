Read the Legend Before the Row

Four Labels, Four Sources

Each color marks a source and a changeability level — never whether an item is good, bad, authentic, or verified.

Ultramarine

Visible in the sheet

Only what the spreadsheet currently shows. A label, not a fact about the item itself.

Vermilion

Visible after opening

What you can only see once you open the link and land on the actual page.

Moss green

Likely to change

Price, stock, options, images, seller, and shipping wording — all prone to shifting.

Soft violet

Confirm at the destination

Facts you must re-check on the real external page before you rely on them.

Margin bracket

The color is a reading aid, not a claim. A moss-green price is simply a price that can change — not a bad price, not a good price, and not a price anyone has verified.

A Four-Step Row Reading Sequence

Read the Row in Order

A proof-sheet routine that works even when the columns are unfamiliar.

Read what the sheet shows

Start with the row label and the cells that are visible right now.

Open the link

See what appears only after you open it — the page, the images, the options.

Mark what can change

Flag price, stock, options, images, seller, and shipping as changeable.

Confirm at the destination

Re-read the facts on the actual external page before relying on any of them.

Reading example — not live data, verification, or a saved record.

Labels Are Not Proof

A Label Is a Pointer, Not a Verdict

The legend helps you read a row. It never turns a row into evidence.

1What a label gives you

  • A starting place to browse.
  • A way to sort what you see by source.
  • A clearer idea of what to re-check.

2What a label cannot give you

  • Proof that the item is authentic.
  • A guarantee of quality, price, or stock.
  • Any confirmation beyond what the page shows.
The Changeable Detail Rail

Six Details That Can Change

Treat these as moving parts. Read them where they live, at the moment you need them.

Price

Currency, amount, and discount wording can shift between views.

Stock

Availability changes without notice and is not a promise.

Options

Colors, sizes, versions, and quantities may differ from the row label.

Images

Photos can be replaced, re-staged, or shown for a different variant.

Seller

The storefront or listing owner can change over time.

Shipping

Times, costs, and service wording are estimates until confirmed.

This is a static list of the kinds of details that change — not live status, not a tracker, not a monitor.

Re-read at the Destination

Before You Rely on Anything

The sheet is a starting pointer. The destination page is where facts get confirmed.

  • 1

    Open the actual page. Re-read the detail where it lives, at the moment you need it.

  • 2

    Treat the sheet as a pointer. It is not a saved record of a page that may have changed.

  • 3

    If a detail changed, re-read it. Do not carry the old value forward silently.

  • 4

    Never turn a color into a verdict. The legend sorts sources; it does not certify anything.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an AllChinaBuy spreadsheet?

An AllChinaBuy spreadsheet is a shared document that compiles listing references, category groupings, and product links for use with agent shopping services. It is a pointer to browse and re-check, not an official, live, or complete product catalog.

Is this website the official AllChinaBuy service?

No. This website is an independent informational and navigation resource. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or operated by AllChinaBuy or Google.

What do the four legend colors mean?

Each color marks a source and a changeability level, never a verdict. Ultramarine marks what is visible in the sheet, vermilion marks what appears only after opening a link, moss green marks details likely to change, and soft violet marks facts to confirm at the destination.

Do the colors mark items as good, bad, or verified?

No. The colors describe only where information comes from and how likely it is to change. They do not indicate whether an item is good, bad, authentic, or verified.

How do I read a row when the column structure is unknown?

Use the legend as a four-step sequence: read what the sheet shows now, open the link to see what appears only then, mark the details that can change, and re-confirm facts on the actual external page before relying on them.

Where do I confirm price, stock, and options?

On the actual destination page, at the moment you need the information. The spreadsheet is a starting pointer; price, stock, options, images, seller, and shipping wording can all change and must be re-read where they live.
